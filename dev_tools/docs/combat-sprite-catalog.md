# Combat Sprite Catalog

The [Combat Sprite Catalog](../combat_sprite_catalog/README.md) is a standalone
Godot tool for inspecting original and replacement combat art. Its **Monsters
and NPCs** tab lists creature records and additional combat-icon pairs, shows
ready/action images alternating every second, and edits catalog metadata. Its
**Character sprites** tab previews composed character bodies, weapons, shields,
and colors. The catalog loads original game art locally; its replacement PNGs
are reviewed here but are not generated by the catalog.

From the repository root, launch it with:

```bash
./dev_tools/combat_sprite_catalog/run.sh [POOLRAD_DIRECTORY]
```

The game-data directory is required. The launcher uses `OPENGOLD_GAME_DIR` or
`~/POOLRAD` by default and accepts `GODOT_BIN` for a different Godot executable.

## Files and workflow

- [`json/`](../combat_sprite_catalog/json) has one saved record per monster, NPC, or extra icon. Records hold descriptions, source identity, equipment, wealth, regeneration flags, and image variants. **Save monster data** writes edited metadata here.
- **Sprite size (combat squares)** sits above the description in the monster editor. Choose **1 × 1**, **1 × 2 tall**, **2 × 1 wide**, **2 × 2**, or **3 × 3**, then press **Save monster data**. The selected `sprite_size` is stored in that record's JSON as `width_squares` and `height_squares`. **Unknown** stores `null` when a source cannot establish the size. Copied monsters inherit the chosen size and can change it independently.
- **Humanoid height** sits below Sprite size in the monster editor. Choose **Small**, **Normal**, **Tall**, or **N/A (non-humanoid)** and press **Save monster data**. JSON stores the choice as `humanoid_size`: `small`, `normal`, `tall`, or `na`. Copies inherit it and can change it independently.
- [`art/`](../combat_sprite_catalog/art) holds replacement `ready.png` and `action.png` pairs under each source key and weapon-set variant. Missing pairs fall back to locally decoded original art. See the [art inventory](../combat_sprite_catalog/art/README.md).
- One-handed weapons pair with available shields; ranged and two-handed weapons have separate sets. Include unready weapons when planning art. Each set needs its own reviewed ready/action pair.
- The [generation and review workflow](../combat_sprite_catalog/generation/README.md) and [rules](../combat_sprite_catalog/generation/rules.txt) govern new sprite art. Keep body scale and planted foot/base coordinates fixed between frames; review the actual 128 x 128 images alternating in the catalog before clearing a regeneration flag. The [pose guide](../combat_sprite_catalog/art/pose-guide/README.md) covers the approved bipedal attack poses.
- [`godot/`](../combat_sprite_catalog/godot) contains the standalone UI and workflow script; [`native/`](../combat_sprite_catalog/native) contains its Godot extension.

The [tool README](../combat_sprite_catalog/README.md) describes original-art
bindings, metadata editing, character previews, and the `--catalog-check` and
`--metadata-check` commands. The generation workflow documents its separate
`prepare`, `check`, and `accept` commands and visual acceptance requirements.

## How sprite sizes were assigned

The catalog reads the game's current combat-icon binding and both original poses.
The game's [CPIC decoder](../../src/OpenGold.Formats/src/formats.cpp) preserves
each image's pixel width and height; assembled character icons use the game's
24 × 24 character compositor. One combat-art square is 24 × 24 original pixels.
The larger dimension of the two poses determines the initial dropdown choice:
24 × 24 → 1 × 1; 24 × 48 → 1 × 2 tall; 48 × 24 → 2 × 1 wide; and 48 × 48 →
2 × 2. The saved choice is editable and is not overwritten on load.

All 199 current monster, NPC, and extra-icon records had a decodable ready/action
pair: 158 are 1 × 1, 20 are 1 × 2 tall, 19 are 2 × 1 wide, and 2 are 2 × 2.
There are no records whose **displayed source sprite dimensions** could not be
determined. Some creature-to-icon matches are labeled candidate or shared in
the catalog, so those sizes describe the currently displayed icon rather than
proving a unique historical icon for the creature.

The saved footprint must match the **decoded game art**, including both poses.
The 199 current records have the same distribution as the source-icon audit
above. In particular, ogres, trolls, ettins, efreeti, minotaurs, hill giants,
and fire giants use 24 × 48 art and therefore have a 1 × 2 sprite footprint.
Tyranthraxus and the wyvern use 48 × 48 art and have a 2 × 2 footprint. Do not
convert SRD Large/Huge creature spaces into sprite dimensions: those rules
describe controlled ground area, not the source image's width and height.

The game's [current battlefield renderer](../../src/OpenGoldBox/godot/scenes/combat_battlefield.gd)
fits ordinary visible monster art inside one cell, regardless of source image
dimensions. The dropdown records the **game-art sprite footprint** for art
preparation; it does not change game occupancy or rendering. The game code was
read for this audit and was not modified. Run the read-only
[source-size audit](../combat_sprite_catalog/godot/scripts/source_size_audit.gd)
after changing footprint JSON; it checks every saved size against both decoded
original poses and fails if any record disagrees:

```bash
OPENGOLD_GAME_DIR="$HOME/POOLRAD" /Applications/Godot_mono.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot \
  --headless --path dev_tools/combat_sprite_catalog/godot \
  --script res://scripts/source_size_audit.gd
```

## Fixed combat-square preview

The monster/NPC preview has a permanent **3 × 3 dotted grid**. Each square is
128 logical pixels; its origin, cell boundaries and bottom ground line stay at
the same coordinates when switching records, weapon sets, and ready/action
poses. The selected `sprite_size` shades a footprint starting at the lower-left
square. The art is centered over that footprint and registered by the ready
frame's planted-foot/base row. The same scale and base anchor are used for the
action frame, so a badly registered pair visibly jumps instead of being
recentered independently. The tool window is 1600 × 1000 to retain the grid,
editor and source details together.

Replacement PNGs use one scale per height tier, never a fit-to-image transform:
Small 1.0, Normal 1.45, Tall 2.1. Non-humanoids use the larger side of the saved
footprint as their canvas scale. Original icons use their source pixel dimensions
at 24 pixels per square, with Small originals reduced to 70% for the height
comparison. These are **canvas display scales**, not assertions that every old
painting already has the right body height. The tool reports when opaque pixels
fall outside the selected footprint; correct the art or its intended footprint
instead of silently shrinking a frame. Changing a dropdown immediately updates
the preview, but saving is still required to write the JSON.

Run the read-only [art-fit audit](../combat_sprite_catalog/godot/scripts/art_fit_audit.gd)
to list every installed replacement pose that crosses its selected footprint:

```bash
OPENGOLD_GAME_DIR="$HOME/POOLRAD" /Applications/Godot_mono.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot \
  --headless --path dev_tools/combat_sprite_catalog/godot \
  --script res://scripts/art_fit_audit.gd
```

The audit counts both poses of every weapon-set variant, reports missing
replacement poses that currently fall back to original art, and lists each
installed pose that crosses its selected footprint. The resulting list is an
art-correction queue, not an animation approval or a reason to recenter either
frame.

## Humanoid height and art consistency

`humanoid_size` is a **visual height tier**, separate from `sprite_size` and
from the SRD creature-type tag. The [SRD 5.2.1 creature-size rules](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf)
name Tiny, Small, Medium, Large, Huge, and Gargantuan; the catalog's simpler
Small/Normal/Tall choices group these for art direction. A goblin or kobold is
Small, human-scale figures are Normal, and an ogre or hill giant is Tall.
This includes humanoid-shaped creatures even when the current SRD calls their
game type Fey, Dragon, Giant, Undead, or another type. Non-humanoid body plans
use N/A. The tiers do not change tactical occupancy or creature rules.

The initial values for the 199 saved records are 14 Small, 143 Normal, 18 Tall,
and 24 N/A. Comparable SRD stat blocks or species entries informed the values
where available. Named Pool of Radiance characters, Quicklings, the giant
skeleton, and forms without exact SRD matches use the catalog description and
currently bound art as visual evidence. Tyranthraxus's dragon-form icon is N/A;
its separate humanoid-form icon is Normal. These are editable art-direction
choices rather than inferred rule statistics.

For humanoid art, measure apparent body height from planted feet to the head,
without counting weapons, raised arms, horns, or effects. Small figures must
read shorter than Normal figures, and Tall figures taller; specifically,
goblins < human-scale fighters < hill giants. Keep the same body height and
planted-foot coordinates in ready/action frames and every weapon set for a
creature. The [generation rules](../combat_sprite_catalog/generation/rules.txt)
carry the saved tier into each prompt, and the review checklist requires a
visual height comparison. The agreed visual targets on the fixed grid are about
**0.6 square** for Small, **0.9 square** for Normal, and **1.7 squares** for
Tall, measured foot to head. A footprint includes the complete sprite and its
equipment, while the height target measures only the body. Existing PNGs are
not automatically resized or treated as correct merely because their canvas is
128 × 128.

For every named leader, compare its body against the regular creature of the
same kind in the same game archive (and against other regular records of that
kind when the archive has more than one). The leader must read as **a little
larger and more imposing**, never smaller: aim for a modest increase in
foot-to-head height and body mass while retaining the same humanoid height tier.
Distinctive clothing and gear may reinforce the rank, but a larger weapon,
helmet, horns, or raised arm cannot substitute for a larger body. Check both
ready and action poses and **every weapon set** at the fixed catalog zoom.
Preserve the game-derived `sprite_size` and keep the complete silhouette inside
its footprint. When a regular sprite already fills that footprint, correct its
art scale as needed rather than enlarging the leader beyond the allowed tiles.
